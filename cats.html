Feeding guidance by life stage

Cats were first domesticated around 7500 BCE in the western Asia region and are currently the second most popular domestic pet in the United States. Care and diet can differ between breeds, but some practices are broadly recommended.

Zero to Four Weeks

It is important the queen, a term commonly used for a female cat that is either pregnant or nursing, directly nurse her young if possible. Monitor kitten growth closely to make sure the growth rate is progressing steadily. If a kitten is not growing at a sufficient rate, a caretaker might need to feed the kitten directly with a bottle or feeding tube.

Possible reasons for poor weight gain

  • Too many siblings competing for mom's milk
  • Gastrointestinal disease
  • Environmental conditions such as extreme heat or cold, or unsanitary conditions

Four Weeks to One Year

Kittens can start being introduced to soft wet kitten food typically around three to four weeks after birth. According to the ASPCA, kittens at this age should eat half to one cup of dry kitten food or six to nine ounces of wet kitten food per day. If your kitten has difficulties eating hard food, a small amount of water can be added to soften the food.

Cat food optimized for kittens provides additional nutrients needed for growth, energy, and wellness. Depending on breed, your cat may have different dietary requirements. Always consult your veterinarian for recommendations.

One Year to Seven Years

This is when kittens reach the cat stage and do not need as many nutrients. Activity and metabolism decrease. It is not recommended to leave food out for cats all day. Instead, provide food a couple times a day so they eat meals rather than snack throughout the day. This practice reduces the risk of obesity and other weight-related feline ailments.

Seven Years and More

Much like many living organisms, the body begins to deteriorate and experience many changes. Cats at this age should eat less fat and calories and more quality proteins. When purchasing packaged food, look for food that states a particular protein, such as "salmon," rather than only a broad category such as fish.
